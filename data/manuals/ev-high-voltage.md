# Electric Vehicles and High-Voltage Systems

Internal reference for service advisors.

**Where we do this work:** EV-specific jobs are only offered at Seattle Downtown,
Portland East, Austin North and Chicago Loop. Denver, Atlanta and any other site can
handle tyres, brakes and cabin filters on an EV, but not high-voltage work. Check
availability before promising a booking.

**Safety rule:** only technicians with high-voltage certification work on orange-cabled
components. Nobody removes a service disconnect, opens a battery pack, or works on
charging hardware without that qualification. This is not a house style preference —
these systems can be lethal.

## What customers report

| Symptom | Likely meaning |
|---|---|
| Range noticeably lower than last winter | Usually normal cold-weather behaviour |
| Range down year on year | Gradual capacity loss — measure before alarming them |
| Charging much slower than usual | Cable, charger, temperature, or a port fault |
| Car will not accept a charge at all | Charge port, lock actuator, or onboard charger |
| Warning about reduced power, "turtle" symbol | Protection mode; needs a diagnostic |
| Completely dead, will not wake or charge | Very often the 12V battery, not the pack |
| Rattle or whine from under the car | Reduction gear or suspension, not the pack |

## The jobs

- **EVB — battery health check, 60 min.** Reports usable capacity against the original
  figure, cell balance, and any stored battery faults. This is the report customers
  want when buying or selling a used EV.
- **HVI — high-voltage system inspection, 90 min.** Insulation resistance, cable and
  connector condition, coolant circuit for the pack, and stored fault codes.
- **EVC — charging port repair, 120 min.** Port assembly, lock mechanism, and the
  wiring back to the onboard charger.

## Setting expectations on battery degradation

Some capacity loss is normal and not a fault. A useful rule of thumb is a few percent in
the first year, then a slower decline. A car showing around 90% of original capacity
after three or four years is behaving as designed, not failing.

What accelerates it: routinely charging to 100% and leaving it there, frequent rapid DC
charging, and long periods parked at very high or very low charge. The advice worth
giving is to keep it between roughly 20% and 80% for daily use, and charge to full only
before a long trip.

Winter range loss is different — the energy goes into heating the cabin and battery,
and it returns when the weather does. Nothing is wrong with the car.

## Charging basics for advisors

- **AC charging**, home or workplace, goes through the car's onboard charger and is
  limited by it. A customer whose home charging is slow may simply have a car with a
  modest onboard charger.
- **DC rapid charging** bypasses that and is limited by the battery's temperature and
  state of charge. Rapid charging slows dramatically above about 80% — this is
  protective behaviour, not a fault.

## What is the same as any other car

Tyres, brakes, suspension, cabin filters, wipers, 12V battery and alignment are ordinary
work on an EV, and the first four are where most EV servicing actually happens. See
[tyres.md](tyres.md), [brakes.md](brakes.md) and [battery-12v.md](battery-12v.md).

## Escalation

Physical damage to the pack, a car involved in a collision, any burning smell, or a
battery fault under manufacturer warranty goes to the franchised dealer. Do not attempt
a diagnostic on a pack suspected of physical damage.
