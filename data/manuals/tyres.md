# Tyres, Alignment and Balancing

Internal reference for service advisors.

## Reading wear patterns

Wear tells you what is wrong before any measurement does.

| Pattern | Cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Both edges worn, centre fine | Chronically underinflated | Pressure check, then monitor |
| Centre worn, edges fine | Overinflated | Pressure correction |
| One edge only | Camber or toe out of specification | Alignment (ALN) |
| Feathered, sharp across the tread blocks | Toe misalignment | Alignment (ALN) |
| Patchy or cupped | Worn shock absorbers | See [suspension.md](suspension.md) |
| Flat spot in one place | Heavy braking, or standing for months | Usually replacement |

## Depth and pressure

- Legal minimum is 1.6 mm across the central three-quarters. We advise replacement at
  3 mm, because wet braking distance deteriorates sharply below that.
- Check pressures cold, monthly, against the plate in the door shut or fuel flap — not
  the number on the tyre sidewall, which is a maximum rather than a recommendation.
- Raise pressures for a fully loaded car or a long motorway run if the plate lists it.

## Symptoms and what they mean

| Symptom | Likely cause |
|---|---|
| Vibration through the wheel around 80–110 km/h | Wheel out of balance (BAL) |
| Vibration through the seat, not the wheel | Rear wheels out of balance |
| Car drifts on a flat, straight road | Alignment (ALN), or uneven pressures |
| Steering wheel off-centre when driving straight | Alignment after kerb impact |
| Rhythmic humming that rises with speed | Tyre wear pattern, or a wheel bearing |

## The jobs

- **ROT — rotation, 30 min.** Every 10,000 km, to even out the faster front wear.
- **ALN — four-wheel alignment, 60 min.** After kerb strikes, new suspension parts, or
  whenever wear is uneven. Alignment is the cheapest way to protect a set of tyres.
- **BAL — balancing, all four, 45 min.**
- **PUN — puncture repair, 30 min.** See the rules below.
- **TFT — tyre fitting, 30 min per tyre.**

## When a puncture cannot be repaired

We will not repair, and should say so plainly:

- Damage in the sidewall or shoulder, rather than the central tread area.
- A cut longer than about 6 mm.
- A tyre already run flat, showing scuffing inside.
- Most run-flat tyres, unless the maker explicitly permits repair.
- Any tyre below the legal tread depth — it is being replaced anyway.

Tyres are replaced in axle pairs where possible. Mixing a new tyre with a badly worn
one on the same axle unbalances braking.

## Electric vehicles

EVs are heavier and deliver torque instantly, so tyres wear faster — expect noticeably
shorter life than an equivalent petrol car. Many require specific load-rated tyres, and
fitting the wrong rating is both unsafe and an insurance problem. Check before quoting.

## Escalation

Vibration that survives correct balancing points at a buckled wheel, a failing bearing,
or a driveshaft. Book a diagnostic rather than rebalancing repeatedly.
