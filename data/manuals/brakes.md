# Brake System Service

Internal reference for service advisors. Written for this service centre; not a
manufacturer procedure. Always follow the vehicle maker's figures where they differ.

## What customers report

| Symptom | Usual meaning | Urgency |
|---|---|---|
| High squeal when braking | Wear indicator touching the disc — pads near end of life | Book within a week |
| Grinding, metal on metal | Pads worn through; the backing plate is cutting the disc | Do not drive |
| Pedal sinks slowly under steady pressure | Fluid leak or failing master cylinder | Do not drive |
| Spongy or long pedal travel | Air or moisture in the fluid | Urgent |
| Steering wheel pulses when braking | Uneven pad deposits or disc thickness variation | Book normally |
| Car pulls to one side | Seized caliper or collapsed flexible hose | Urgent |
| Handbrake will not hold on a slope | Cable stretch or rear caliper fault | Book normally |

## What we check (BIN, 30 min)

- Pad thickness at all four corners. New pads are 10–12 mm. We advise replacement
  below 3 mm and refuse to sign off below 2 mm.
- Disc thickness against the minimum stamped on the disc hub, plus scoring and lip.
- Brake fluid moisture content. Above 3% the fluid boils too easily under hard use.
- Flexible hoses for bulging, cracking and chafing.
- Caliper slide pins for free movement, and even pad wear side to side.

## The jobs

- **BPF / BPR — front or rear pads, 90 min.** Pads are replaced in axle pairs, never
  one wheel alone. We clean and grease the slide pins and bed the new pads in.
- **BDR — discs in pairs, 120 min.** Required when discs are below minimum thickness,
  deeply scored, or cracked. Always fitted with new pads.
- **BFL — fluid flush, 45 min.** Recommended every two years regardless of mileage.
  Brake fluid absorbs water from the air, and wet fluid boils during long descents,
  which gives a pedal that goes to the floor exactly when it is needed most.

Fronts wear roughly twice as fast as rears on most cars, so a customer who has only
ever had fronts done is not being neglected.

## Notes by vehicle type

- **Electric and hybrid:** regenerative braking does most of the slowing, so pads
  often last much longer. The trade-off is corrosion from light use — seized calipers
  and rusted disc faces are the common EV brake fault, not wear.
- **SUVs:** heavier, so expect shorter pad life than a hatchback driven the same way.

## Safety and escalation

Grinding noises, a pedal that sinks, or a visible fluid leak mean the car should be
recovered rather than driven in. Offer the earliest slot at any branch and say plainly
that driving it is unsafe. Never quote a firm price for brake work before inspection:
discs often only reveal their condition once the wheel is off.
