# 12V Battery and Charging System

Internal reference for service advisors. Applies to every vehicle we service,
including electric ones.

## What customers report

| Symptom | Usual meaning |
|---|---|
| Slow, laboured cranking | Battery losing capacity, often age related |
| Single click, then nothing | Flat battery, poor terminal connection, or starter solenoid |
| Rapid clicking | Not enough current to hold the starter engaged — usually the battery |
| Dash lights dim while cranking | Battery under strain |
| Needed a jump start more than once | Battery at end of life, or something draining it overnight |
| Battery-shaped warning light while driving | Charging fault, not the battery itself — suspect the alternator |
| Electrics behave oddly, radio resets | Low voltage or a loose earth strap |

## Tests before quoting

| Test | Healthy reading |
|---|---|
| Resting voltage, engine off 30+ min | 12.6 V or above. Below 12.4 V is partly discharged |
| Voltage while cranking | Stays above 9.6 V |
| Charging voltage at idle | 13.8–14.4 V |
| Parasitic drain, car asleep | Under 50 mA on most vehicles |

A battery that tests fine but keeps going flat points to a drain, not a battery. Selling
a battery in that case means the customer is back within a fortnight, angrier.

## The jobs

- **BAT — battery replacement, 30 min.** Includes terminal cleaning and, where the
  vehicle needs it, registering the new battery to the charging system. Skipping
  registration on a car that expects it shortens the new battery's life.
- **ALT — alternator, 180 min.** For charging voltage outside range, or a whining
  noise that rises with engine speed.
- **STR — starter motor, 150 min.** For a confirmed no-crank with a good battery.
- **HLB — headlight bulb, 20 min.** Small job, but dim lights are sometimes a low
  voltage symptom rather than a failing bulb.

## Typical causes of short battery life

- Short journeys that never fully recharge the battery.
- Cold weather: capacity drops sharply near freezing, which is why failures cluster
  in the first hard frost.
- Accessories wired in badly, especially dashcams left on permanent power.
- Age. Most batteries manage three to five years.

## Electric vehicles

EVs have a conventional 12V battery as well as the traction pack, and it runs the
computers that let the car wake up. A flat 12V battery in an EV looks alarming — the
car may be completely dead and unable to charge — but it is an ordinary, cheap fix.
Never confuse it with the high-voltage pack: see [ev-high-voltage.md](ev-high-voltage.md).

## Escalation

If the car is not at one of our sites and will not start, we do not recover vehicles.
Advise a jump start or roadside assistance, then book the diagnostic.
